## 1. A refund left from the wrong account

Owner refunded a client's deposits in full today — AED 63,000 to Neeraj Mohan Sharma — and
the whole amount came out of the **bank** while the **cash** account did not move.

Both payout paths hardcoded `bankCode: "1002"`:

| Where | File |
|---|---|
| `handleRefundFull` — the *Refund all* button | `deposits.jsx` |
| the refund half of `handleCloseout` | `deposits.jsx` |

So whichever account a deposit was *received into*, the refund always left Mashreq 1002.

**Why it hid.** Both sides of the movement look correct in isolation: 2230 is debited by
what was refunded and nets back to zero, and the totals on the Client Deposits page —
Received, Refunded, Still Held — are all right. Only the *split between the asset
accounts* is wrong: cash stays overstated by the refunded amount and the bank understated
by the same. Nothing on the deposits page can show that, because the page never looks at
which asset account moved.

### The fix

Two pure helpers in `core.jsx`, next to `buildClientFundsLedger`:

- **`clientFundsHeldByAccount(party, accounts)`** — where a party's held money physically
  sits, per bank/cash account, in fils. A receipt adds its cash/bank debit to that
  account; a payout takes its credit back off it.
- **`clientFundsRefundSplit(party, accounts, refundCents, fallbackAccountId)`** — splits a
  refund across those accounts, one leg per account.

`deposits.jsx` now posts **one payout per account** through the existing
`postClientFundsPayout`, each with that account's own `bankCode`. Nothing else about the
posting changed. Mashreq 1002 is used only as the fallback for money that cannot be traced
to an account at all.

Three ordering decisions, each picked deliberately:

1. **Commission earned at close-out is drawn from the earliest money first.** That entry
   carries no cash line by design, so nothing in the ledger says which account the kept
   commission came out of. First-in-first-applied is the readable convention.
2. **A refund is drawn from the most recently funded account first** — the mirror of the
   above, so whatever is *not* refunded stays on the earliest money and a later read of the
   same party agrees with itself.
3. **An account driven negative is netted against the others.** This is exactly what an
   already-mis-sourced refund looks like: cash +63,000, bank −63,000. Netting keeps the
   per-account figures summing to what is actually held, so the old error cannot produce a
   phantom balance to refund twice.

The refund and close-out modals now name the account before you confirm — *"Paid out of
Cash (1001)"*, or both accounts with their amounts on a split refund. That is the part
that would have caught this by eye.

## 2. "If I delete it from the bank, does that reverse the refund?"

Checked rather than assumed. The Banking **Delete** button (`pages.jsx:3103`) calls
`window.fsDeleteDoc` — a **hard delete**. Its own confirm box says "No reversal will be
created."

So yes, it fully undoes the refund: the bank goes back from 145,660.69 to 208,660.69, the
AED 63,000 shows as held again, the deposit returns to the open list with its *Refund all*
button, and receipts RCP-00001 / RCP-00002 are untouched.

This is **not** the contra-entry path used by *Reverse*. It leaves no trace that a wrong
refund was ever posted. The alternative offered — one correcting journal moving the 63,000
back into the bank and out of the account the deposits came into — gives the same
balances *with* an audit trail. Owner's call. Warned that the fix had to be live first,
or the second refund would come out of the bank again.

## 3. PAID stamp and client refund acknowledgement on the receipt voucher

Owner wants the client to sign for money handed back. The Security Deposit Receipt now
doubles as that acknowledgement once the deposit is settled.

`DepositReceiptDoc` takes a new optional **`refund`** prop. When present it renders:

- a green rubber-stamp **PAID**, with **REFUNDED** (or **SETTLED**, where commission was
  deducted) and the payout date beneath it;
- a **Refund Acknowledgement** block: a first-person sentence naming the client, the amount
  in figures *and* words, the date, the account it was paid from, the commission deducted
  where there was one, and a no-further-claim line — over **Client Name / Client Signature /
  Date** lines.

Both sit inside `#dep-receipt-doc`, so they come through on **Download PDF**.

`refundInfoFor(receipt)` in `DepositsPage` decides whether a receipt is settled, and it
gates on the **party**, not the receipt: `held === 0 && refunded > 0`. A deposit still
held, or only partly refunded, gets no stamp — and neither does a voided receipt, which
keeps its VOID mark.

| Case | Amount acknowledged |
|---|---|
| Refunded in full, nothing deducted | **this receipt's own amount** — Neeraj's two receipts say 35,000 and 28,000, not 63,000 |
| Commission deducted at close-out | the party's refunded total, with the commission named, flagged as a total where the party has several receipts |
